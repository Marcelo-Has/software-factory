# Playbook — editorial

> **Skeleton — matures with the first real use.**
>
> This file exists so the category has a place and a name before it has content. It records
> the obvious emphases and, more importantly, **what's still undecided**. A skeleton is
> **not** authority: until it matures, what applies is `factory/docs/CRAFT-PRINCIPLES.md` (the
> floor) and `.claude/rules/design-antipatterns.md` (always), plus the project's own
> `DESIGN.md` (identity).
>
> **Category:** reading surface — article, documentation, publication, long-form text.
> See `factory/docs/playbooks/README.md`.

---

## Obvious emphases

- **Typography is the structure, not the finish.** Here the typographic system (craft §2) does
  the work that in other categories is done by boxes, borders, and color. If the page needs a
  container to stay organized, the typography isn't resolved.
- **Reading rhythm:** line-height, paragraph spacing, and the distance between heading levels
  form a single cadence across the whole page. More space **above** a title than below it — a
  title belongs to what comes after it (craft §3).
- **Line measure between 45 and 75 characters**, held across all three breakpoints. It's the
  constraint that defines the reading column, and it's what decides the grid — not the other
  way around.
- **Heading hierarchy is the document's structure**, never a size choice: no skipped level, and
  the same structure serves navigation, a table of contents, and a screen reader.
- **Inline elements** — quotes, a captioned image, a note, code, a table — get a declared
  treatment and **don't break** the column's rhythm when they appear.
- **Low density and the reader's time** (craft §7): nothing should compete with the text while
  it's being read. Scroll-triggered motion, cascading entrances, and marquees are especially
  hostile here.
- **Contrast and comfort for long reading:** AA is the floor; body text never below 16px; no
  all-caps running text and no justified text.
- **Emphasis without switching families**: italics, weight, and space do the job; a serif word
  inside a sans-serif title doesn't.

## What's still undecided

- `design-critic`'s extra rubric: how to measure "reading rhythm" over a screenshot, and how
  much real text the critic evaluates against.
- Category gates: reading time/depth, a table of contents and anchors, printing, the browser's
  reading mode, `article`/microdata.
- Image-in-text policy — allowed widths, bleed, caption as its own typographic role in
  `DESIGN.md` §4.5.
- Notes, references, and citations: one consistent pattern for the whole product.
- Fluid typographic scale (`clamp`) vs. discrete steps, and which one this category prefers.
- Reading theme (light/dark/sepia) — a context-of-use decision (craft §4) or a product one?
- How this category composes with **mobile** (long-form reading on a small screen is the
  majority case).
- Relationship with narrative content generated by product-level skills, where an em-dash is
  a feature of the language, not of the LLM (an already-declared exception to that
  anti-pattern).

## How this skeleton matures

With the **first real project** in the category: what the Foundation had to decide that wasn't
written down, and what the critic found post-render that was characteristic of the category,
become sections 1–4 in the format of the complete playbooks
(`institutional-marketing.md`, `saas-dashboard.md`). Maturing the skeleton is its own issue's
work, not the work of the PR that used it for the first time.
